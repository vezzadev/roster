## Problem Statement

Pedro is solo-building Roster (Docker-compose for human+AI teams) to maximalist standards: TDD iron law, four test layers, subagent-driven implementation, demo evidence on every PR, human approval gate. The repo is already public with full notes. He wants to adopt a build-in-public practice that simultaneously (a) dissolves any residual inhibition to show incomplete/imperfect work and (b) creates credibility for an acquihire-shaped outcome — primary target Anthropic, secondary AI2, tertiary other AI labs. Mechanism is path-agnostic: noticed-and-hired, literal acquihire, contract, or fellowship.

## Demand Evidence

**Self-reported as "vibes, not tested."** Pedro has not specifically verified that build-in-public produces acquihire outcomes for solo technical builders targeting AI research labs. That admission is itself a founder signal — most founders confabulate evidence; he named the gap.

External evidence assembled during this session (via Codex web search):

- Anthropic's own careers page lists at the top of the resume: independent research, insightful blog posts, substantial open-source contributions.
- Recent hires with strong public-work-to-hire pipelines: Karpathy (Axios, May 19 2026), Jan Leike (TechCrunch, May 2024), Akari Asai at AI2 (2025-2026).
- Anthropic technical staff public footprint follows a consistent pattern: paper + blog/tweet thread tied to executable artifact (Trenton Bricken, Petri, MCP).
- AI2 release pattern is even more codified: paper + technical blog + data + code + eval code + demo (Tulu 3, OLMo, OLMoTrace, Molmo).

**Pedro's first-hand evidence (added post-Codex):** he has direct contact with Anthropic engineers and reports that Amodei's essays (Machines of Loving Grace and related) are treated as gospel internally. Two implications: (a) there is already a warm sourcing path that does not require pure cold outbound; (b) the cultural genre that resonates is the Amodei-style narrative arc on capable agents and containment — not adjacent indie or general SaaS-builder discourse.

**Verdict:** lab engineers read engineering write-ups, evals, papers, code, technical demos. They are anchored on the Amodei narrative arc culturally. Generic indie-hacker tweeting is disproved as the right wedge for this audience.

## Status Quo

Repo is already public. Design doc at `docs/gstack/design.md` (849 lines). README rewritten for humans. AGENTS.md documents conventions. PRs include demo evidence. Commits are thoughtful. **All of this is invisible to the target audience.** Two distinct failure modes:

1. **Reach gap (Pedro's stated primary):** zero inbound traffic from lab engineers. The motivated lab reader has no path to encountering the repo.
2. **Packaging gap (Codex's challenge, partially accepted):** even if a lab engineer landed on the repo, the depth of `design.md` and the project structure don't compress into a 3-minute evaluable unit. Sprawling artifact > legible unit.

Pedro rejected Codex's framing that packaging is the *primary* gap, arguing: "Even with sharp packaging, my repo gets zero lab-engineer traffic right now. The packaging-only argument assumes inbound interest I don't have." Synthesis adopted: packaging and reach are sequential and complementary, not competing.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Target reader:** a staff/senior engineer or researcher at Anthropic or AI2, working on agents, agent safety, evals, multi-agent coordination, or open model infrastructure. Examples (non-exhaustive): Trenton Bricken, Sholto Douglas, the safety-research team behind Petri, AI2 OLMo/Tülu/OLMES contributors.

**What gets them to click:** a technical claim tied to an executable artifact and concise evidence, positioned within the Amodei-style safety arc on capable agents. Not a personality, not a roadmap, not a vision essay. A claim they can audit in 3-5 minutes, framed in language they already share.

**Narrowest wedge:** one lab-grade artifact on Roster's strongest substantive claim — `Auditable Takeover in Multi-Agent Teams: Credential Rotation as a Safety Primitive` — opening with a 2-3 paragraph framing inside the larger containment-of-capable-agents arc (Amodei-style register), then narrowing to the specific primitive. Paired with a daily presence motion in lab-adjacent conversations on X.

## Constraints

1. **Ongoing budget is small; one-time artifact budget is real.** Two different envelopes, conflated in the original framing — surfaced by spec review.
   - **Ongoing:** 15-20 min/day for presence motion. Near-zero maintenance after the artifact ships.
   - **One-time (artifact build):** 30-50 hours over 4 weeks (~8-12 h/week). **This is a real change in work effort and it will slow Roster v1 by ~2-3 weeks.** That tradeoff is accepted as the cost of the strategy; it is not free.
2. **AI delegation allowed.** Pedro is open to extra work specifically when delegated to an AI agent. Roster itself can host parts of the pipeline at v1.5+ (deferred).
3. **Maximalist standards must remain visible.** Process is the artifact. Hiding the rigor loses the signal.
4. **Anti-newsletter, anti-broad-brand-content, anti-weekly-thread.** Codex's "what not to build" list is binding.

## Premises (v3 — final, after Codex challenge and user rebuttal)

1. **The repo is public but invisible. Both reach and packaging need work, with reach as the primary gap and packaging as the multiplier on reach.** Sequential: produce sharp artifact → distribute it deliberately.
2. **Perfectionism is the real blocker. Counter: show the maximalist process working — process becomes the artifact.** Test pyramid, subagent-driven implementation, demo-on-every-PR are the credibility signals.
3. **Roster is already lab-shaped.** Multi-agent coordination + a real safety primitive (takeover by credential rotation) + maximalist eval rigor.
4. **Work-effort budget allows AI delegation.** Roster itself can host the publishing/discovery pipeline at v1.5+, but the first move is not roster-dependent.
5. **Target reading habits are now partially known (Codex diligence).** Lab engineers read engineering write-ups, evals, papers, code, and technical demos. They do not read standalone design docs that lack executable claims. They do not read indie-hacker tweets.
6. **The practice IS the product demo at v1.5+ but not at v1.** First move is one packaged lab note + presence motion. The lab note ships *before* Roster v1 milestone, riding on whatever subset of the takeover primitive is end-to-end runnable at that moment (see Week 0 gate). Dogfooding (D) becomes available as a layer only after both v1 and the lab note ship; it is out of scope for this design.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Presence-first reach
Daily 15-20 min on X engaging substantively with 5-10 lab technical staff. Artifact deferred until presence exists.
**Effort:** S daily, ongoing. **Risk:** Med (slow burn, no concrete signal for months).
**Reuses:** nothing in roster.

### Approach B: One sharp artifact + active distribution (Codex prototype)
Build the credential-rotation lab note in 1-2 weeks. Ship as note + demo + diagram + repro + eval table + failure case. One X post linking everything + 5-10 targeted seeding replies.
**Effort:** M concentrated, then low. **Risk:** Med (one-shot variance).
**Reuses:** existing takeover design in `design.md`, existing test infra, existing demo-on-PR rule.

### Approach C: Parallel presence + artifact (chosen)
Start daily presence motion immediately. In parallel, ship the lab note on a 4-week timeline. Artifact lands on a warmer base.
**Effort:** M+ total. **Risk:** Low (robust to which mechanism actually drives outcomes).
**Reuses:** all of B's reuses + ongoing engagement habit.

### Approach D: Dogfood roster on visibility (out of scope for this doc)
A roster team where agents watch lab Twitter, draft replies, monitor responses. The publishing pipeline IS the demo.
**Effort:** L upfront. **Risk:** High (recursive — couples visibility to roster ship date).
**Reuses:** roster itself.
**Decision:** out of scope. Re-evaluate only after both Roster v1 and the lab note have shipped. Not mentioned further in this doc.

## Recommended Approach: C (parallel presence + artifact)

### Why C over A, B, D

- **vs A (presence only):** A defers the artifact indefinitely. With no shipped lab-grade artifact, the substantive signal never materializes, even with strong presence. Pedro has the substance (takeover-by-credential-rotation is a real primitive). Withholding it is the perfectionism trap.
- **vs B (artifact only):** B accepts Codex's "packaging is primary" framing that Pedro rejected. Shipping the artifact into a void with no warm audience risks the launch flopping silently. Pedro's "zero lab traffic right now" critique applies.
- **vs D (dogfood roster):** D couples visibility timeline to Roster v1 ship date. The practice should be available now, not in N months. D is a v1.5+ unlock.

**Lab note artifact (4-week timeline):**

### Why this lands

- **Anthropic careers page literally asks for this:** "independent research, insightful blog posts, substantial open-source contributions." The lab note is all three at once.
- **AI2's standard pattern is paper + blog + code + eval + demo.** The lab note mirrors that structure with smaller scope. AI2 contributors will recognize the shape.
- **The substantive claim is real.** Takeover-by-credential-rotation is a non-trivial safety primitive for multi-agent systems. It's not a toy demo or a wrapper. It's something a safety researcher could cite.
- **Process visibility is preserved.** The eval table (10+ takeover drills) is the maximalist-rigor signal made legible. The four-test-layer regime is referenced in the failure case section.
- **Amodei-register intro lowers the cognitive cost for the target reader.** The framing maps the primitive into the language Anthropic engineers already share. That is not flattery — it is choosing a basis vector the reader is already calibrated on, so the technical contribution lands faster.
- **First-hand contact path exists.** Pedro can plausibly seed the note to a known Anthropic engineer for honest critique before public release, which both strengthens the artifact and pre-warms the launch.

## Open Questions

1. **Which specific replies actually move the needle?** Unknown until tried. Logging the presence motion lets you A/B reply styles after 4-6 weeks of data.
2. **Does Twitter/X remain the right channel through 2027?** Lab engineer activity is migrating somewhat to Bluesky, lobste.rs, GitHub Discussions. Re-evaluate at the 3-month mark based on where target engagement actually concentrates.
3. **Will the credential-rotation primitive hold up to scrutiny?** The lab note will get critiqued. That's the point. Plan to publish a v2 incorporating real critique, not delete the v1 on the first negative reply.
4. **What's the bail-out condition?** If by month 4 there are zero meaningful signals (no replies from target accounts, no inbound DMs, no warm intros, no application-relevant feedback), revisit the strategy. The session premise is "vibes, not tested" — month-4 review is where the test concludes.

## Success Criteria

**Tier 1 — Practice held (controls perfectionism):**
- Daily presence motion: at least 5 substantive replies per week, sustained for 12 weeks. Missed days are allowed; quitting is not.
- Lab note shipped within 4 weeks of starting (by 2026-06-16).

**Tier 2 — Signal received (validates strategy):**
- Lab note: ≥1 reply or quote-post from someone at Anthropic, AI2, or an adjacent lab within 4 weeks of publication.
- Inbound: ≥1 unsolicited DM or email from a lab engineer within 8 weeks of publication.

**Tier 3 — Outcome conversion (validates the goal):**
- ≥1 conversation with a lab hiring contact within 6 months of the lab note shipping.
- Within 6 months: at least ONE of (a) a formal interview loop initiated, (b) a paid contract / consulting conversation started, (c) an explicit no-go from a sourced contact with reasoning. (c) counts because it converts "vibes" into a real signal — the goal of the test is to learn, not to win on the first try.
- Within 12 months: an actual offer or collaboration, or a clear pivot decision with documented reasoning.

Failure of Tier 1 = perfectionism won. Failure of Tier 2 with Tier 1 success = the artifact was wrong; iterate on packaging or claim. Failure of Tier 3 with Tier 2 success = the goal needs reframing; the practice itself is still net-positive. Zero data at Tier 3 by month 6 (no conversations, no nos) = the funnel is broken upstream; revisit distribution and target list, not the artifact.

## 3. Industry Structure: Fragmentation

### Key Structural Assessment
The Indonesian logistics sector is structurally fragmented:
- Owner-operator truckers (1–5 trucks per firm) dominate land transport
- EMKL/freight forwarding is similarly fragmented, with most operators being single-port or single-route specialists
- **Conventional methods dominate**: manual booking, phone/WhatsApp-based dispatch, paper PODs, no real-time tracking

**Source:** Waresix CEO public statement, Waresix About page, accessed 2026-05-23
URL: https://www.waresix.com/tentang-kami/

> "Ketidakpastian adalah masalah utama dalam logistik Indonesia. Masing-masing komponen industri masih terfragmentasi, ruwet, dan menggunakan metode konvensional yang tidak efisien. Hal ini menimbulkan permasalahan di sepanjang proses logistik, mulai dari ketidakpastian pesanan, waktu dwelling time yang lama, hingga tidak transparannya informasi status pengiriman."
> (Translation: "Uncertainty is the main problem in Indonesian logistics. Each industry component is still fragmented, complicated, and using conventional inefficient methods. This causes problems throughout the logistics process, from uncertain orders and long dwell times to opaque shipment status.")

**⚠️ GAP:** The exact number of registered freight forwarding companies (SIUJPT license holders) has not been confirmed from a primary source this session. BPS and Kemenhub pages were inaccessible. Working estimate from prior reports is ~4,000–6,000 licensed freight forwarding firms, but this requires primary source confirmation.

### ADB Confirmation of High Informality
The ADB's April 2026 Indonesia assessment explicitly identifies transportation as a high-informality sector where structural transformation has lagged:

> "Sectors absorbing the largest share of workers remain dominated by informal employment... Similar patterns are in wholesale and retail trade, **transportation**, and other services, where informal employment continues to dominate."

This has direct implications: many logistics SMBs operate informally, lack digital infrastructure, and may not be reachable through formal channels.

**Source:** ADB, *Asian Development Outlook April 2026*, Indonesia Economy page, accessed 2026-05-23
URL: https://www.adb.org/where-we-work/indonesia/economy

## 5. Key State-Owned Logistics Players (SOE Sector)

### Danantara "Super Logistics" Consolidation (2026 — High Relevance)
In a major structural development, **Danantara Indonesia** (Indonesia's sovereign wealth fund) announced plans in May 2026 to consolidate **15 SOEs and their logistics arms into a single integrated logistics entity**. This is the most significant recent structural change in the Indonesian logistics sector.

**Entities included:**
- **Holding company (profitable):** Pos Indonesia
- **Other profitable SOEs:** Pelindo Terminal Petikemas, ASDP Indonesia Ferry, Pelni, KAI Logistik, Integrasi Logistik Cipta Solusi
- **Loss-making SOEs being absorbed:**
  - Pupuk Indonesia Logistik: losses of **Rp 90.52 billion (~$5.24M)** in 2024
  - Semen Indonesia Logistik: losses of Rp 30.29 billion in 2024, **widening to Rp 188.35 billion in 2025**

**Market share context:** Pos Indonesia is already losing consumer parcel market share to J&T Express and Shopee Express — private-sector digital platforms are winning against the state incumbent.

**Source:** The Jakarta Post (Tenggara Strategics analysis), "Analysis: Danantara bets on logistics merger, stronger firms may foot the bill," published May 4, 2026, accessed 2026-05-23
URL: https://www.thejakartapost.com/opinion/2026/05/04/analysis-danantara-bets-on-logistics-merger-stronger-firms-may-foot-the-bill.html

> "Danantara Indonesia has announced plans to consolidate 15 state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and their logistics arms into a single 'super' logistics entity in an effort to address longstanding structural issues in Indonesia's state-owned logistics sector. The consolidation spans multiple segments, from railway distribution to fertilizer distribution, and combines both profitable and loss-making firms under the ambition of building a more integrated and efficient national logistics backbone."

> "Despite losing market share in the consumer parcel business to on-demand delivery services such as J&T Express and Shopee Express, [Pos Indonesia's] profitability remains supported by regulatory advantages."

**Strategic implication for client:** The Danantara consolidation affects the large, SOE-owned side of the logistics sector, not the SMB freight forwarding / 3PL segment that is the client's ICP. The private SMB segment (Waresix's customer base, ALFI members) is not directly affected. However, the consolidation signals that the Indonesian government is actively trying to reduce logistics inefficiency — which could increase openness to digital tools.

## 6. Key Private-Sector Digitization Signal: Waresix

Waresix — the most prominent Indonesian logistics-tech platform — is a useful proof point:

**Company overview:**
- **Founded:** 2017 (as warehouse management)
- **Expanded to:** Trucking (2019), port operations/first-mile (2020), end-to-end logistics (2021)
- **Recent milestones:** Container depot in Surabaya (2023), ISO 27001:2022 certification (2024), "NextGen Tech 30" award from Granite Asia (2024)
- **SaaS layer:** "Waresix Operating System (WOS)" — ML/AI-powered TMS with smart matching, real-time analytics, e-Invoice, e-POD
- **Customers:** GoKomodo (Supply Chain Director), Sociolla, Heinz ABC
- **Blog active:** As recently as September 2025
- **CEO:** Andree Susanto

This confirms: (a) there is demand for TMS/logistics-tech among Indonesian shippers and 3PLs; (b) the incumbent solution is an **operator-SaaS hybrid** (Waresix bundles its own freight capacity with the software), not a standalone TMS — leaving room for a pure-SaaS vendor targeting SMBs who want software without operator lock-in.

**Source:** Waresix website (homepage + About page), accessed 2026-05-23
URLs: https://www.waresix.com/ and https://www.waresix.com/tentang-kami/

## 7. World Bank Logistics Performance Index (LPI)

**Critical methodology note:** The World Bank **redesigned the LPI in 2025** from a perception/survey basis (old LPI) to shipment-level tracking data (LPI 2.0). The 2025 report covers 2023–24 data. Direct comparison to old LPI scores is not valid.

- Under the old LPI methodology, Indonesia ranked approximately **#63 out of 139 countries in 2023** — this is from prior secondary knowledge and has NOT been confirmed by a live primary source this session. ⚠️ Flag for verification.
- The new LPI 2.0 approach tracks maritime partners, aviation partners, container dwell times, and postal delivery times rather than survey-based perceptions.

**Source:** World Bank LPI homepage + Connecting to Compete 2025, accessed 2026-05-23
URLs: https://lpi.worldbank.org/ and https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099042226142014971

> "Connecting to Compete 2025 introduces the Logistics Performance Indicators 2.0 (LPI 2.0), a fundamental redesign of the World Bank's former survey-based Logistics Performance Index toward a system grounded in shipment level data."
> Document Date: 2026/04/22

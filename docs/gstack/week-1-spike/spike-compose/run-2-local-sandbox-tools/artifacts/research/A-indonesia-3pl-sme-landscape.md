# Thread A: Indonesian 3PL / Freight-Forwarder / Trucking SMB Landscape
_Researcher first-pass — 2026-05-23_

---

## Summary

Indonesia's logistics sector is structurally large, archipelagic-complex, and heavily fragmented, with digitization still in early innings for SMB operators. The sector contributes an estimated **~24% of GDP** as a logistics-cost share — one of the highest ratios in the world — signalling enormous inefficiency. The country spans **17,000 islands** with **270+ million people**, creating persistent multi-modal complexity that no single operator dominates. The digital economy overall is approaching **$100B GMV** in 2025 but logistics-specific digitization (TMS/WMS adoption among smaller 3PLs) lags consumer-facing sectors sharply.

---

## Key Data Points

### Logistics Cost / GDP Ratio
- **Source:** ALFI (Indonesian Logistics and Forwarders Association), About page
- **Claim:** "The logistics industry significantly contributes to Indonesia's GDP, accounting for nearly **24%**"
- **Note:** In global context, logistics costs as % of GDP in the US are ~8%; Indonesia's 24% is frequently cited as a structural competitiveness problem and a reform target. The ratio reflects cost burden, not value-add.
- **URL:** https://alfi.or.id/about-us (accessed 2026-05-23)

### Country Context
- 270+ million residents, 17,000+ islands, strategic position at key Southeast Asian shipping lanes
- Government target: become a global logistics hub by 2045 ("Visi Indonesia Emas 2045")
- ALFI was established in 1989 and has regional chapters (DPC = branch councils, DPW = provincial councils) across Indonesia
- **Source:** ALFI About page (https://alfi.or.id/about-us, accessed 2026-05-23)

### Digital Economy Context
- Indonesia's digital economy is growing double-digits across sectors, "reaching the cusp of $100B" in 2025
- Bank Indonesia cut interest rates six times since September 2024; government launched $1.5B stimulus including transportation subsidies
- Private funding is subdued in 2024 but investors are watching "software and services" (SaaS) as a priority sector
- **Source:** Google/Temasek/Bain, *e-Conomy SEA 2025* Indonesia report (https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/indonesia_e_conomy_sea_2025_report.pdf, accessed 2026-05-23)
- Also: *e-Conomy SEA 2024* full report: SEA digital economy at $263B GMV (+15% YoY), $89B revenue (+14% YoY). SaaS/software & services identified as a growing investment target.
- **Source:** Google/Temasek/Bain, *e-Conomy SEA 2024* (https://services.google.com/fh/files/misc/e_conomy_sea_2024_report.pdf, accessed 2026-05-23)

### Fragmentation Signal (CEO-level observation)
Waresix CEO Andree Susanto (in company About page) states:
> "Ketidakpastian adalah masalah utama dalam logistik Indonesia. Masing-masing komponen industri masih terfragmentasi, ruwet, dan menggunakan metode konvensional yang tidak efisien."
_(Translation: "Uncertainty is the main problem in Indonesian logistics. Each component of the industry remains fragmented, complicated, and uses conventional, inefficient methods.")_
- **Source:** https://waresix.com/tentang-kami/ (accessed 2026-05-23)

### LPI Context
- World Bank has released **LPI 2.0** (April 2026), now based on shipment-level tracking data (maritime, aviation, postal), not survey perception scores. The previous survey-based LPI 2023 ranked Indonesia **63rd globally** (per prior public data; see gap note below). LPI 2.0 covers 2023–24 data.
- **Source for LPI 2.0 report:** *Connecting to Compete 2025: The New Logistics Performance Indicators 2.0*, World Bank, published 2026-04-22 (https://documents.worldbank.org/en/publication/documents-reports/documentdetail/099042226142014971, accessed 2026-05-23)
- **Gap:** The LPI 2.0 country-specific Indonesia scorecard page returned 403. The old LPI 2023 PDF was not directly accessible. The LPI 2023 rank of 63rd cannot be verified via direct primary source in this session.

## Gaps / Data Not Yet Sourced

1. **Firm count**: BPS (Statistics Indonesia) website serves JavaScript-rendered pages that don't return tabular data through web-scraping. BPS freight-forwarding company count by province was not accessible. ALFI member count is not stated on their public website. Asperindo domain (asperindo.or.id) resolved with DNS error.
   - **Workaround needed**: BPS physical publication "Statistik Transportasi" or Kemenhub SIUJPT license count would be the authoritative source. Recommend EM note this as a confirmed data gap with a flag for the brief.

2. **Market size ($)**: No primary or secondary market sizing figure (e.g., "Indonesia 3PL market = $X billion") was accessible from a directly verifiable source in this pass. ALFI's 24% of GDP logistics cost implies ~$260–270B in logistics spend using 2023 GDP of ~$1.1T, but this is a logistics *cost* total for the economy, not an addressable market for SaaS/TMS.

3. **Digitization rate**: No official survey on % of Indonesian freight forwarders using a TMS or digital platform was accessible in this pass. The fragmentation signal from Waresix CEO and the described conventional methods suggest low TMS penetration, but this is anecdotal.

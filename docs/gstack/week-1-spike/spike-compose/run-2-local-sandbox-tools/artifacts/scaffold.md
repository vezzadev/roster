# Brief scaffold — decision logic before evidence arrives

## The question
US vertical SaaS for logistics SMBs ($1–10M ARR). Should they enter Indonesia in 2026? If yes, how?

## What "enter" could mean (rank-order of cost/commitment)
1. **Opportunistic / inbound-only** — keep ID inbound leads, accept self-serve sign-ups, do nothing local. Cost ~ $0 incremental.
2. **Channel partner** — sign a local SI / consultancy reseller. Maybe one US-based BD person owns the partner. ~$150–300K/yr.
3. **Light direct (rep-on-the-ground)** — 1–2 Jakarta hires (sales + CS), contractor or EOR, no entity. ~$300–600K/yr.
4. **PT PMA + small team** — local entity, 3–6 people, PSE-registered, IDR billing. ~$800K–1.5M/yr year-one + capital.
5. **Acquisition** — buy a struggling local TMS/freight-forwarder-software player. Variable.

For a $1–10M ARR firm, options 4–5 are existential bets. The brief should treat them as such.

## Recommendation decision tree (working hypothesis — to be tested by evidence)
- **GO + light direct (option 3)** IF: addressable SMB segment >5,000 firms with budget; no dominant local incumbent already locking up the segment; PSE/PDP compliance is tractable; ARPA can be set at ≥$3K/yr without piracy substitution.
- **GO + channel partner (option 2)** IF: segment exists but GTM friction is high (language, relationship-driven sales, fragmented buyers).
- **DEFER / opportunistic only (option 1)** IF: incumbents have already consolidated the SMB segment (e.g., Waresix/Kargo own logistics SMB software mindshare), or regulatory cost-to-comply >12 months of expected revenue, or local WTP collapses unit economics.
- **NO-GO (don't enter)** IF: addressable SMB count <1,000 firms with budget, OR three years of foreign-SaaS exits with no offsetting wins.

## Evidence that would flip the recommendation
| Hypothesis | Evidence needed to confirm | Evidence that flips it |
|---|---|---|
| Segment is large enough | ALFI ~3,800+ freight forwarders; ≥10,000 trucking SMBs digitizing | <1,500 firms with internet/ERP adoption |
| Local incumbents are beatable | Waresix/Kargo pivoted away from SaaS-for-3PLs, or focus on shippers not 3PLs | One incumbent has >30% share of mid-market 3PL TMS |
| WTP supports US-equivalent ARPA | Comparable SaaS sells $2–5K/yr to ID SMBs | All ID logistics SaaS is freemium / take-rate |
| Regulatory cost is tractable | PSE registration takes weeks not months; no forced localization for non-PSE-public | Mandatory data localization for logistics data; opaque PDP enforcement |
| FX/macro is manageable | IDR stable 14k–16k; inflation <5% | IDR depreciation >15%/yr or capital controls |

## Brief structure (working — will revise)
1. **Recommendation** (one paragraph, unambiguous)
2. **Why** — 3 bullets, each citing the strongest evidence
3. **What the market actually looks like** — segment sizing, fragmentation
4. **Competitive reality** — named players, what they do, where the gap is
5. **The regulatory / operational tax** — PSE, PDP, FX, talent
6. **Recommended entry mode + 18-month milestones**
7. **Kill criteria** — what we'd see at month 6/12 that means pull out
8. **Open questions / what we couldn't source**

## Tone / quality bar
- Every non-trivial claim cited with URL + date.
- No "Indonesia is a large and growing market" generic filler. Specific firms, specific regulations, specific numbers.
- If evidence is thin, say so — don't paper it over.

## Source target

**Decision (2026-05-19, pre-spike): Indonesia market-entry brief for a mid-market vertical-SaaS firm targeting Indonesian logistics SMBs (3PL / freight forwarders, $1M-$10M ARR). Question for the brief to answer: should they enter? If yes, how?**

- **Industry / market the brief covers:** Indonesian B2B SaaS for the logistics SMB segment. Specifically: a hypothetical US-based vertical-SaaS firm (think mid-market TMS / freight-OS category) considering entry into Indonesia in 2026.
- **Why this target:**
    1. **Geographic adjacency to comparator without copy-risk.** BCG Vietnam is the primary rubric anchor. Indonesia is the natural neighbor — Southeast Asia entry frame transfers, but the target country is different, so agents can't lift the brief structure verbatim from the comparator.
    2. **Plentiful public data.** Google-Temasek-Bain "e-Conomy SEA," McKinsey ASEAN, World Bank, and Indonesian trade press all cover this market — agents have credible sources to cite.
    3. **Not adjacent to AI/agent domain.** Avoids the recursion problem where the agents would be briefing on their own market. Lets the founder grade neutrally based on whether the analysis would convince a SaaS strategy buyer.
    4. **Real decision space.** Plausible outcomes range from "do not enter — TAM does not justify GTM cost" to "enter via local reseller partnership" to "acquire a local player." The brief has to defend its recommendation, not stamp it.
    5. **Mid-market bounding.** "Mid-market vertical-SaaS for logistics SMBs" is specific enough to constrain the brief but not so narrow it's contrived.
- **Public comparator brief:** BCG, "Vietnam: A Global Engine of Growth" (2023) — same SEA geography frame, different target country. See [t1-grading-rubric.md](t1-grading-rubric.md) "Anchor" for the full comparator set.
